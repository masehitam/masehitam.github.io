Privacy Policy

Last updated: 18 October 2026

YTLive is a desktop application for managing 24/7 live streams to YouTube from servers that you own and operate (your own computer or VPS, called a “Worker”). This policy explains what data YTLive accesses and how it is handled.

1. Data we access from Google

When you click Connect YouTube, you sign in with Google and grant YTLive the https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube permission. YTLive uses it only to:

  • read the basic details of your own channel (channel ID and title) to show which channel is connected;
  • create live streams (stream keys) and live broadcasts on your channel, start them, and end them, when you start a broadcast or a schedule you created in YTLive.

YTLive does not read, modify, or delete your videos, comments, subscribers, analytics, or any other data.

2. Where your data is stored

  • The Google OAuth refresh token is sent directly from your computer to your own Worker and stored there encrypted (AES-GCM). It is not stored on your computer.
  • Stream keys are stored encrypted on your Worker and are never written to logs.
  • The developer of YTLive does not operate any server that receives your data. No data is sent to the developer or to any third party. YTLive contains no analytics, advertising, or tracking.

3. Sharing

YTLive does not sell, rent, transfer, or share your data with anyone. Data is only exchanged between your computer, your Worker, and Google/YouTube APIs, as required for the features above.

4. Google API Services User Data Policy

YTLive’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. YTLive also uses YouTube API Services; by using these features you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service and acknowledge the Google Privacy Policy.

5. Revoking access and deleting data

  • In YTLive: Channels → Disconnect deletes the token from your Worker.
  • At any time: revoke YTLive’s access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Removing the Worker (or its data folder) deletes all stored data, because it exists only there.
